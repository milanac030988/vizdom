1. Summary

The three end-to-end grounders click the right element more often than VizDOM: Aria-UI by 8 points over the OmniParser and modular detectors, ELAM and UI-TARS by 5 to 6, and all of them by 11 to 14 over UIED. On the test-oracle question the picture inverts. Asked whether a button that is not on the screen is visible, Aria-UI says PASSED in 88 of 88 cases, ELAM in 86 of 88 and UI-TARS in 60 of 88. VizDOM never does, because its locator contract requires exactly one match before anything is reported present. VizDOM's own verify errors are all in the conservative direction: a present caption that OCR could not read, reported as FAILED.

Two things are new in this run. The modular backend now ties the OmniParser monolith on every one of the 514 steps, at 61 % of its parse time, which closes the gap diagnosed in the run 2 report. And the same fix lifted UIED by 9 click steps. The OmniParser column itself is identical to run 2 on all 514 steps, on a different card, so every VizDOM change is accounted for by the code change.

Run 4 answers the objection that the table compares VizDOM without its language tier against grounders that are all language tier. Resolving the same steps as descriptions (desc="Save button") through the production chain, lexical scoring over the DOM with a 3B text model as tie-breaker, lifts VizDOM to action-hit 0.930, ELAM's figure, and the best verify accuracy of the table, 0.954, while passing 1 of the 88 absent targets. The model tiers pinned alone do not hold up: the 3B text model over the whole DOM reaches 0.634 and the 3B vision model with the Set-of-Mark overlay 0.117, the latter mostly a harness limit. Run 5 put the vision model behind the lexical shortlist, as production does: it keeps the hits (0.930) and passes 34 of the 40 absent targets it was asked, so the vision tier is not an oracle and stays out of the default chain (section 9).

Reading for the thesis. A GUI test oracle must not pass a check whose target is absent. That is the one failure class the lifecycle benchmark isolates with the verify-neg steps, and it is the one where the architecture difference shows: 0 for a resolved-DOM system against 0.20 to 0.29 for per-step vision-language grounding, at one fifteenth to one twenty-sixth of the inference cost per step on this suite. The best grounder at clicking, Aria-UI, is also the worst oracle: it never says no.

Outcome of the 301 verify steps per executor

213 statements are true (the element is on screen), 88 are false (the label does not exist on that screen). Each bar sums to 301. ▲ A longer blue segment is better; the green "false pass" segment is the one to minimise, and 0 (no green at all) is the only acceptable value for a test oracle. Orange false fails are cheap: the test stops and a person looks.

correct verdictfalse fail (true statement, said FAILED)false pass (absent target, said PASSED)errored (run 5: Ollama refused the call, no verdict)

The negative statements use labels that cannot exist on a business dashboard: Frobnicate, Warp Cores, Zylophone, Moon Cheese, Purple Elephant, Antigravity, Bogus Panel, Quantum Sync. The statement is of the form "the 'Frobnicate' button is visible", expected verdict FAILED.

  • ELAM uses its native verdict mode, the model-card prompt that asks it to think step by step and conclude PASSED or FAILED. It concluded PASSED on 296 of 301 statements, including 86 of the 88 impossible ones. Reading its outputs, it rationalises the unknown label onto a nearby real element and then confirms visibility. The two negatives it rejected were Warp Cores and Moon Cheese on two login screens; in run 2 it rejected one. It also produced three false fails on true statements (form_sidebar_0031/E28, settings_0002/E5, settings_0194/E15).
  • UI-TARS has no verdict mode; the benchmark proxies the verdict by whether the model grounds the target to a point. It grounded 60 of the 88 nonsense labels to some element and never refused a true statement. It did reject Frobnicate 13 times out of 17, so the behaviour is label-dependent rather than random; it also rejected every negative on the dashboard family except four.
  • Aria-UI (addendum run) uses the same proxy as UI-TARS, and it returned a point for every one of the 301 statements: all 213 true ones (no false fail) and all 88 impossible ones. A false-pass rate of 0.292 is the ceiling of this metric: 88 of 301. The model that grounds best is the one that never declines to ground, which is exactly what makes it unusable as an oracle without a second mechanism.
  • VizDOM resolves the locator against the parsed DOM. An absent label has zero matches, so the verdict is FAILED by construction. Its 20 errors are the OCR-unreadable captions above, and they are false fails: the test would stop and a human would look, which is the cheap failure. A false pass is the expensive one, because the suite stays green while the UI is broken.

The ratio depends on how many steps a suite runs against each screen. Here it is 514 steps over 44 states, about 12 steps per state, and VizDOM-OmniParser is 15 times cheaper per step than Aria-UI, 26 times cheaper than UI-TARS and 39 times cheaper than ELAM; the modular backend is 26, 44 and 65 times cheaper. A suite that visits each screen once would narrow the gap to parse time against inference time, 1.0 s against 1.6 to 2.7 s. Regression suites revisit the same screens constantly, which is the regime the comparison is about. Aria-UI's per-step figure comes from a faster card with 1.4 TB/s of memory bandwidth and only 3.9 B active parameters per token, so it is the cheapest grounder here despite being the largest model; on the run 3 host it would likely sit between the other two.

Across the two hosts the VizDOM columns got about twice as fast (the 6000 Ada and a faster CPU for the CV stages), ELAM got faster per step (5.15 → 4.00 s) and UI-TARS slower (1.68 → 2.72 s). ELAM's verify steps take longer than its actions (median 4.3 s vs 3.3 s) because the verdict prompt reasons before concluding. Neither adapter changed between the runs, so the UI-TARS shift is a host effect; the grounders' per-step cost should be quoted as a range across cards, not as one number.

8. What run 3 settles: the Step 6b2 fix and run-to-run stability

The run 2 report traced the modular backend's 15-point deficit to a schema mismatch: absorbed OCR text lands in text for the modular backend and in label for the monolith adapter, and the pipeline's own OCR pass adds the value a second time, so a value-bearing input field produced two elements carrying the same literal. The fix, pipeline Step 6b2 (ADR-010), drops a text element that sits inside a control carrying the same literal. Run 3 is the first GPU run with it.

The production chain: better than text= on the same DOM, and still an oracle

  • 198 hits against 188: eleven gained, one lost. Every gain is a caption that exact text cannot match but token overlap plus the role word can: the placeholder Search..., which OCR reads without the dots (seven steps on data_table, form_sidebar and toolbar), and the path-like values APAC\USER01 and C:\Users\admin (partial overlap, lexical scores 1.7 to 2.6). The one loss is a table digit, data_table_0110/E80 "2 button", where the margin between repeated digits is zero. Verify accuracy follows the same pattern, 0.954 against 0.934.
  • The lexical tier does the work. 398 of 514 steps are answered without a model, in 0.2 ms. The text model is called on the other 116, the 88 negatives plus 28 positives with no confident lexical top, and answers 3 of them; the whole column spends 16 s on resolution. The 13 remaining action misses are the OCR floor, nine captions that were never read so there is no token to overlap (Save three times, Confirm, Cancel, Download, three value fields on form_sidebar_0103), and four repeated table digits.
  • One false pass. Asked about "Purple Elephant button" on dialog_0141, the text model answered the Retry button at confidence 0.85, the same rationalisation the grounders show; it declined the other 87 negatives. Action-hit now equals ELAM's 0.930, with a false-pass rate of 0.003 against the grounders' 0.199 to 0.292. The root cause, found on 2026-10-28 by reproducing the step with the same model on a laptop, is a shortlist of one: the dialog's other button, Cancel, is one of the nine captions OCR never read, so the lexical ranking scored exactly one element and the model was handed a one-row table, which turns "which of these matches?" into "is this it?". With the same phrase and two rows the model declines. The resolver now pads the shortlist to at least three candidates and tells the model that null is expected and a matching role alone is no match; the runner keeps every parsed DOM in the tarball so the next such case can be read off directly.

The model tiers alone

  • The 3B text model over the whole DOM is a poor grounder. Given the full element table (30 to 110 rows) it picks a wrong element on 49 of its 78 action misses, at confidence 1.00 and semantically unrelated ("Prev button" → a static text John Doe; "Search... input field" → the number 70), and declines on 29. Worst on form_sidebar (33 of 62) and settings (33 of 57), fine on toolbar (16 of 17) and login (11 of 12). Its six false passes are rationalisations ("Zylophone button" → the Zip Code button, "Warp Cores button" → the Time button, all at 1.00). What makes the production chain a 0.930 column is the lexical tier in front: it answers the easy 77 % itself and hands the model a shortlist of eight.
  • The VLM-only column measures the harness more than the model. The Set-of-Mark overlay draws at most 20 numbered boxes in DOM order, and the pinned vlm chain has no lexical shortlist in front of it, so the marks are the first 20 elements of a DOM that has a median of 33 elements per state (5 to 112); on a laptop parse of the same states only 102 of the 213 action targets fall inside the first 20 marks at all. On 30 of the 44 states the model gave no usable answer on any step (null, out-of-range or below the confidence gate; the result file keeps the chain's "no confident match", not the tier's own reason). On the 14 states where it did answer, all four toolbars, six settings screens and four dashboards, it answered almost every step: 25 of 45 actions hit, and all 23 negatives it was asked passed at confidence 0.95 to 1.00. Within its answered set it behaves exactly like the grounders. The intended head-to-head with Aria-UI, ELAM and UI-TARS therefore did not take place: the tier was designed as a disambiguator over a short candidate list, and this column asked it to be a whole-screen grounder with a 3B model on a 1920×1080 frame. Both harness gaps were closed in the kit the same day: a vizdom-modular-desc-vlm-chain column runs the vision tier behind the lexical shortlist, as production does, a missed step records every tier's own verdict in its detail instead of the chain's generic line, and a tier that cannot reach its model is a step error rather than a miss.
  • Run 5: the vision tier behind the shortlist keeps the hits and fails as an oracle. The lexical,vlm chain hands the vision model the 116 steps the lexical tier cannot settle, with at most eight marks on the overlay. Two things happened. For two minutes in the middle of the column (11:19:22 to 11:21:13 UTC) Ollama answered HTTP 500 to every call, 68 of the 116: 10 actions, 10 positive and 48 negative verifies. The error path records them as errors, scored as misses and wrong verdicts, so the row is a partial measurement; under the run 4 harness those 48 negatives would have counted as correct and the column would have looked better than it is. On the 48 calls that went through, the model behaved like the grounders. Asked about a nonsense caption with a numbered overlay in front of it, it answered a mark on 34 of 40 negatives at confidence 0.90 to 0.99, 22 of them mark #1, and declined 6 (all on dashboards and toolbars). Of its four action answers one hit (settings_0042 "Save button", mark #6 at 1.00), two chose mark #1 for the same description on other settings screens, and one was out of range. The chain therefore keeps the lexical tier's 198 hits (one gained, one lost to the outage) and loses the oracle: false-pass 0.113 as measured, which is also the floor had every errored negative been declined, and about 0.25 had they followed the answered rate. The action-side question, whether the vision model recovers the OCR floor of nine unread captions and four repeated digits, is still open: 10 of those 14 steps fell inside the outage. The verify-side question is closed: a Set-of-Mark prompt with a null option is not a usable oracle with this model, and vlm stays out of the production default.

Reading for the thesis. With its language tier switched on, VizDOM clicks as well as ELAM (0.930), verifies better than any column (0.954) and passes 1 absent target in 88, against 60 to 88 for the grounders. The ADR-022 design, deterministic scoring first and a small model only where the scoring is not confident, is what produces that: the model is consulted on 23 % of the steps and overruled by the DOM's id validation and the confidence gate on the rest. The caveat is the phrasing: the benchmark's descriptions are caption plus role word, the lexical tier's ideal input; a tester's own wording ("the blue save button at the bottom") would exercise the model tiers more and the lexical tier less, so 0.930 is an upper bound for this phrasing, not a general desc= accuracy. Run 5 adds the second half of the ADR-022 argument: the tier order matters. The same vision model that is a 0.117 column alone and an 0.113 false-pass column behind the shortlist is harmless in the default chain only because it is not in it; the text model, consulted on the same 116 steps, declines 87 of 88 nonsense captions where the vision model accepts 34 of 40.

10. Threats to validity

  • The desc= descriptions are generated, not written. Caption plus role word is exactly what the lexical tier scores, so the chain's 0.930 (section 9) is an upper bound for that phrasing. The VLM-only column is capped by the overlay's 20-mark budget without a shortlist and does not measure the vision model's grounding ability on this suite. The run 5 column that does put the shortlist in place lost 68 of its 116 vision calls to a model-server outage, so its action-side figure is partial; its verify-side verdict rests on the 40 negatives that were answered and is not.
  • Synthetic screens. All 44 states come from the project's generator. Real applications have denser, noisier layouts; the headline caveat of the whole benchmark stands.
  • Negative statements are easy for exact text and hard for a VLM in different ways. A nonsense label can never partially match a DOM, which favours VizDOM. A model that reads the screen might reasonably be expected to reject "Zylophone", yet mostly did not. A harder negative set (plausible-but-absent labels such as "Export" on a screen with "Import") is planned as defect injection and would test VizDOM's OCR as well.
  • UI-TARS's and Aria-UI's verify is a proxy. Neither has a verdict mode, so "grounded a point" counts as PASSED, and Aria-UI always grounds. ELAM's native verdict is the fairer comparison, and it still passes 86 of 88 absent targets, so the proxy is not what drives the finding.
  • Two runs, two cards, no seeds. Greedy decoding still moved the grounders by 3 and 6 steps between cards, so their figures carry about one point of uncertainty; there is no estimate across sampling seeds. The VizDOM columns reproduced exactly.
  • Verify accuracy for VizDOM is capped by OCR, not by the DOM logic. The 20 misses are the same 20 captions in all three detector columns and in both runs.
  • Latency is host-dependent. UI-TARS was 60 % slower per step on this host than on the 4090 host with the same code; per-step cost should be quoted as a range.
  • Aria-UI comes from a different rental. Its mixture-of-experts weights are not quantized by bitsandbytes (a 24 GB card OOMs at shard 5 of 12 in 4-bit, extrapolating to ~53 GB), so the column runs bf16 and the gate refused the 48 GB card of run 3 as designed. It was measured on a 96 GB Blackwell card two hours later at commit 411873c, with the adapter unchanged. Its accuracy rows are comparable (same manifest, same scoring); its latency row is not (different card, host and CUDA build, and Aria's remote code ran without grouped_gemm).
