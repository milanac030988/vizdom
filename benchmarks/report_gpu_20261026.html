1. Summary

The two end-to-end grounders click the right element more often than VizDOM: by 5 to 6 points over the OmniParser detector and by 16 over UIED. On the test-oracle question, the picture inverts. Asked whether a button that is not on the screen is visible, ELAM says PASSED in 87 of 88 cases and UI-TARS in 58 of 88. VizDOM never does, because its locator contract requires exactly one match before anything is reported present. VizDOM's own verify errors are all in the conservative direction: a present caption that OCR could not read, reported as FAILED.

On cost, VizDOM pays per screen state and the grounders pay per step. For this suite VizDOM-OmniParser spent 110 s in total, which is about 0.2 s per step amortised. UI-TARS spent 1.68 s on every step and ELAM 5.15 s.

Reading for the thesis. A GUI test oracle must not pass a check whose target is absent. That is the one failure class the lifecycle benchmark isolates with the verify-neg steps, and it is the one where the architecture difference shows: 0 for a resolved-DOM system against 0.19 to 0.29 for per-step vision-language grounding, at roughly one tenth of the inference cost per step.

Where the grounders miss

  • ELAM (13 misses) fails on the pagination strip of data tables: the 1, 2, Prev and Next buttons (7 steps), and on status-value input fields such as Enabled or Inactive where the point lands on the label instead of the field.
  • UI-TARS (14 misses) fails on the login screens: the Remember me checkbox and the three social-login buttons on one state, plus a few value fields. Its login family hit rate is 0.58 against ELAM's 0.92.

4. Verification: the oracle row

Outcome of the 301 verify steps per executor

213 statements are true (the element is on screen), 88 are false (the label does not exist on that screen). Each bar sums to 301. ▲ A longer blue segment is better; the green "false pass" segment is the one to minimise, and 0 (no green at all) is the only acceptable value for a test oracle. Orange false fails are cheap: the test stops and a person looks.

correct verdictfalse fail (true statement, said FAILED)false pass (absent target, said PASSED)

The negative statements use labels that cannot exist on a business dashboard: Frobnicate, Warp Cores, Zylophone, Moon Cheese, Purple Elephant. The statement is of the form "the 'Frobnicate' button is visible", expected verdict FAILED.

  • ELAM uses its native verdict mode, the model-card prompt that asks it to think step by step and conclude PASSED or FAILED. It concluded PASSED on 297 of 301 statements, including 87 of the 88 impossible ones. Reading its outputs, it rationalises the unknown label onto a nearby real element and then confirms visibility. The single negative it rejected was Moon Cheese on one login screen.
  • UI-TARS has no verdict mode; the benchmark proxies the verdict by whether the model grounds the target to a point. It grounded 58 of the 88 nonsense labels to some element. It did reject Frobnicate 13 times out of 17, so the behaviour is label-dependent rather than random.
  • VizDOM resolves the locator against the parsed DOM. An absent label has zero matches, so the verdict is FAILED by construction. Its 20 errors are the OCR-unreadable captions above, and they are false fails: the test would stop and a human would look, which is the cheap failure. A false pass is the expensive one, because the suite stays green while the UI is broken.

Why this is structural, not a tuning problem. A per-step vision-language grounder answers "where would this be?" and is trained to always produce an answer. A resolved DOM answers "is there exactly one of these?" and can say no. The benchmark's false-pass column is the first measurement of that difference on identical screens and steps.

6a. Why the modular backend scores below OmniParser

The modular backend is OmniParser taken apart: the same icon-detect YOLO as stage 1, the project's OCR as stage 2, and a line-by-line port of OmniParser's fusion between them. It was expected to tie the monolith column. It is 15 points behind, and the difference is exactly 32 click steps. No step goes the other way: the modular column never hits where OmniParser misses.

Running both backends on form_sidebar_0007.png locally and diffing the two DOMs shows the mechanism. The screen has a form whose fields contain a value. Both backends detect the same field box and the same OCR box for the value, and in both the fusion step absorbs the OCR box into the field, as OmniParser does. They differ in which attribute the absorbed text lands in:

  • The monolith adapter maps every OmniParser "icon" item's content to label and leaves text empty, whether the content was a Florence caption or absorbed OCR. The text= locator searches only the text attribute, so the field is invisible to it and the value text is the single match.
  • The modular backend distinguishes the two cases on purpose: absorbed OCR is a literal read, so it becomes text; only a caption becomes label. That is the more honest schema, but the pipeline's OCR stage then emits the same value again as a standalone text element, and the DOM ends up with two elements carrying the same literal. Nothing deduplicates a text element nested inside an interactable with identical text.
  • It is not the captioner. The sweep ran the modular column with the captioner off (the backend's default; the parity script enables Florence explicitly). Captions only matter for icons without any text, and the toolbar family, where those live, scores the same for all three VizDOM columns.
  • The parity check's "2 labelled vs 13 labelled" on dashboard_0009 is the same representation difference seen from the other side: the monolith puts absorbed OCR in label, the modular backend in text, and the parity script counts label.

Consequence and fix. Detection parity holds: same regions, same OCR boxes, same fusion. The 15-point gap was a schema mismatch in the adapter plus a missing dedup rule in the pipeline. The fix applied after this run keeps the modular backend's more honest schema and adds the rule to the pipeline (Step 6b2): a text element that sits inside a control carrying the same literal is dropped, and the control, the clickable thing, is kept. 7. What changed since run 1 and what is still open

  • VizDOM columns are bit-identical to the 2026-10-25 run on the same box, every step. The pipeline is deterministic on fixed versions. The earlier laptop figures (uied 0.803, omniparser 0.916, verify 0.957) differ only through easyocr, torch and OpenCV versions, so every quoted number carries its commit and machine.
  • ELAM is now a real column. Run 1 produced zeros from a dtype error and then from a wrong prompt template and coordinate scale. With the model-card prompts, percent coordinates and bf16 under autocast it grounds at 0.939. Its verify column had never been measured before this run.
  • UI-TARS moved from 4-bit to bf16 and gained nine action-hit points; verify behaviour barely changed.
  • The modular column's gap is explained and fixed (section 6a); the fix landed after the run, so the corrected column is a laptop number until the next rental.
  • Aria-UI remains unmeasured. The adapter loads up to shard 5 of 12 and then OOMs on 24 GB, because its mixture-of-experts weights are not quantized by bitsandbytes. It needs an A6000 or L40S rental. Until then its column is cited from published numbers.

8. Threats to validity

  • Synthetic screens. All 44 states come from the project's generator. Real applications have denser, noisier layouts; the headline caveat of the whole benchmark stands.
  • Negative statements are easy for exact text and hard for a VLM in different ways. A nonsense label can never partially match a DOM, which favours VizDOM. A model that reads the screen might reasonably be expected to reject "Zylophone", yet mostly did not. A harder negative set (plausible-but-absent labels such as "Export" on a screen with "Import") is planned as defect injection and would test VizDOM's OCR as well.
  • UI-TARS's verify is a proxy. It has no verdict mode, so "grounded a point" counts as PASSED. ELAM's native verdict is the fairer comparison, and it is the worse of the two on false passes.
  • Single run, single card. Generation is greedy, so the grounder outputs are deterministic, but there is no variance estimate across seeds or machines.
  • Verify accuracy for VizDOM is capped by OCR, not by the DOM logic. The 20 misses are the same 20 captions in all three detector columns.
